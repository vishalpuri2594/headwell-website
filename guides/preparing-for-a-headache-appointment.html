How to prepare for a headache appointment

Appointments are short, and headaches are hard to describe on the spot. A little preparation means you spend the time on answers instead of on trying to remember.

2 minute read · Last updated November 2026

Sudden, severe or unusual headache? Get emergency help: call 911, 999, 112 or your local emergency number. See the emergency signs.

In this guide
  1. Before you go
  2. What to tell the doctor
  3. Questions you might ask
  4. After the appointment

Before you go

  • Bring your diary, or a summary of it: how many headache days you have a month, how long attacks last, and how bad they get. See how to keep a headache diary.
  • List everything you take for headaches, including over-the-counter pain relievers, vitamins and supplements, with how often you take them. Include things that didn’t work.
  • Write down what you have already tried, such as medicines, changes to sleep or diet, and what happened.
  • Note any family history of migraine or other headache disorders, and any other health conditions you have.
  • Think about the effect on your life: days of work or school missed, plans canceled, time spent in bed. Some clinics use short questionnaires for this. MIDAS asks about missed or reduced days over the past three months, and HIT-6 is a six-question test of how much headaches affect you.
  • Write down your questions and what you hope to get from the visit, even if it’s just “understand what’s going on”.

What to tell the doctor

Describe a typical attack from start to finish, and then your worst one: where the pain is, what it feels like, what comes with it, how long it lasts, and what you do. Mention anything that has changed recently, anything that frightens you, and how often you reach for pain relief. If you get warning signs or an aura, say so, and say how long they last.

Don’t wait for a routine appointment if you have any of the emergency signs. Get emergency help straight away.

Questions you might ask

  • What type of headache do you think I have?
  • Do I need any tests, and if not, why not?
  • What can I do on the day of an attack?
  • Would a preventive treatment make sense for me?
  • How often is too often to take pain relievers?
  • What should I keep track of between now and my next visit?
  • When should I call you, and when should I go to the emergency room?

After the appointment

Write down what you agreed while it’s fresh: what to try, what to track, and when to come back. If something wasn’t clear, it’s fine to call or message the clinic and ask.
