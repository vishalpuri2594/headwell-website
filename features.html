What HeadWell does

A free tracker for recurring headaches and migraine, for iPhone, iPad and Android. Here is everything it does.

Screens show the iPhone app with sample data.

Log a headache in seconds

Tap the + button and choose what you want to log: a full headache entry, early warning signs, or a quick log. Quick log takes a couple of taps: a pain level from 0 to 10 and where it hurts. Everything else can wait until you feel better.

  • A head map with front and back views: tap the places that hurt
  • More than twenty symptoms, from nausea and light sensitivity to aura and neck stiffness
  • A long list of common triggers
  • Notes, if you want them, and everything except the pain level is optional

Early warning signs

Before the pain, many people notice yawning, a stiff neck, food cravings, a low mood or trouble concentrating. HeadWell lets you log those signs on their own, whether or not a headache follows.

A couple of days later it asks what happened. Your history then shows each warning sign, whether it became a headache or passed, so you can see which signs meant something for you.

  • Choose from a list of common signs or add your own
  • Linking a sign to a headache is always optional

Patterns and your calendar

Your entries turn into a calendar that shows headache days, periods and early warnings together, and into charts of how often attacks come and how severe they’re. A short summary in plain words says what your own log shows, such as which trigger you record most or what time of day attacks tend to start.

  • Headaches per month and average pain over time
  • A pain-free streak counter
  • Choose 3 months, 6 months, a year or everything

Periods and life events

Hormones and life both leave marks on headaches, so HeadWell lets you log them next to each other. Once cycle tracking is on in your profile, record when a period starts and ends and how heavy it’s. Log life events such as travel, an exam, a deadline, an illness or a stressful stretch.

  • Periods: start date, flow, end date and notes, and an import from Apple Health on iPhone
  • Life events in groups: travel and relocation, work and academic, emotional and social, physical and medical, and other
  • Both appear on the calendar, so patterns are easier to spot

Your Home screen

The top of Home shows a colored orb and a line about today, with a button underneath for when a headache starts. Further down are insight cards drawn from your own log, articles picked for you, and a monthly check-in that keeps your profile current.

Forecasts are estimates from your own logged data and, if you allow it, local weather. They can be wrong, and they aren’t a medical diagnosis or treatment recommendation.

A library of articles

A library of articles on migraine, tension headache, treatment, prevention and lifestyle. On iPhone it holds hundreds of articles; Android has the original set of more than forty for now, and the full library arrives with version 2.0.

Browse by category, search by keyword, filter for quick reads or deeper dives, and read offline once they have loaded. Each article opens with three short cards, with the full text one tap away.

Your Journey

Journey, in your Profile, is a progress view for the long run. It looks at how many headache days you’re having, how many good days you have had lately and, if you have filled in the questionnaires, how much headaches affect daily life. Every number comes with an explanation of why you’re seeing it, and a bad stretch doesn’t reset your progress.

It’s a tracking summary, not a diagnosis.

Action Plans

After you log a full headache entry or an early warning sign, HeadWell can show a short list of practical options, such as a breathing session, gentle stretches or protecting your sleep. Each comes with a reason, and many carry a safety note.

They’re options, not obligations. You can choose “Not now” or “Not for me”, and some are prompts to raise something with your doctor. On iPhone you can switch Action Plans off in Profile, under App.

Breathing, stretching and massage

Short guided routines for when you want to do something gentle. Breathing sessions follow a paced circle with a count, and stretching and massage routines walk you through each step with a timer. On iPhone, a figure shows how each step is done.

  • Paced breathing patterns, from about a minute to five
  • Neck and shoulder stretches, singly or as a full routine of just under five minutes
  • Self-massage routines for the temples, base of the skull, forehead, neck and jaw

A report for your doctor

HeadWell can turn your history into a PDF you can print, save or send. It opens with a summary: how many entries, which day and time of day are most common, average pain, how the last month compares with the one before, and your most frequent triggers. After that comes your log.

  • MIDAS and HIT-6 questionnaires are built in, and your scores go into the report
  • A monthly check-in tracks how things are changing, including doctor visits
  • The report leaves out your name and email, but it includes the intake answers and notes you have entered, so look it over before you share it
  • Export your headache log as CSV, or a JSON backup that also includes periods and your MIDAS and HIT-6 results, from Profile, under App

A sample report from a demo account.

Light, dark and Attack Mode

HeadWell follows your phone’s light or dark setting, or you can choose Light, Dark or Attack Mode yourself in Profile, under App, in Appearance. Attack Mode dims the screen for times when bright light is painful.

A few things differ by phone

HeadWell is the same app on iPhone, iPad and Android, with a handful of differences today.

  • Only on iPhone and iPad: Sign in with Apple, importing periods from Apple Health and writing headache events to it, opening an older entry to add details, and switching Action Plans off.
  • On iPhone and iPad now, coming to Android in version 2.0: your own custom triggers, a figure for each stretch and massage step, and the full article library.
  • Only on Android: deleting your account inside the app. On iPhone and iPad, email us instead.

Privacy and control

Your log lives on your phone and syncs to your account, so a lost phone won’t lose your history. Location, Apple Health, Health Connect and notifications are all optional. No ads, no sale of your data, and no third-party analytics or advertising trackers. The Privacy Policy has the details.
