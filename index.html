HeadWell is a free headache and migraine tracking app for iPhone, iPad and Android. You record attacks and early warning signs, mark where it hurts on a head map, and note triggers, periods and life events. Your history becomes a calendar, charts and a PDF report for your doctor. It also has breathing and stretching routines and a library of articles. HeadWell is a self-tracking and education tool; it does not diagnose or treat any condition.

What you can do with it

The essentials. See every feature.

Log a headache in seconds

Tap the + button, choose a pain level from 0 to 10, and tap where it hurts on a front and back head map. Symptoms, triggers and notes are there when you want them, and out of the way when you don’t.

  • More than twenty symptoms to choose from
  • A long list of common triggers
  • Everything except the pain level is optional

Catch the early warning signs

Many attacks announce themselves hours before the pain. Log those early signs when you notice them, and HeadWell checks back a couple of days later to ask whether a headache followed. Over time you can see which signs meant something.

See your patterns

Your entries become a calendar of headache days, with periods and early warnings marked alongside, and charts of how often attacks come and how bad they’re. A short summary says what your own log shows.

Take a clear report to your doctor

HeadWell can turn your history into a PDF report that opens with a summary: how often attacks happen and your most frequent triggers. If you have done them, it includes your MIDAS and HIT-6 questionnaire scores. It leaves out your name and email, and you choose whether to share it.

A sample report from a demo account.

Easy on the eyes

HeadWell follows your phone’s light or dark setting unless you choose. Attack Mode goes further and dims the screen for the times when bright light is painful.

Common questions

What is a migraine?

Migraine is a neurological condition, not just a bad headache. An attack usually brings a throbbing or pulsing pain, often on one side of the head, together with some mix of nausea, sensitivity to light, sound or smells, and tiredness. Left untreated, an attack typically lasts between 4 and 72 hours.

Migraine runs in families and it’s common: roughly one person in seven worldwide has it.

Sources: NINDS: Migraine, ICHD-3: Migraine without aura, The Migraine Trust: What is migraine?

What are early warning signs of a migraine?

Before the pain, some people notice yawning, mood changes, food cravings, neck stiffness, tiredness, trouble concentrating or extra sensitivity to light and sound. These can show up hours, or a day or two, before an attack. This early phase is called the prodrome.

Not every warning sign ends in a headache, which is why it helps to note both the sign and what happened next. Over time some people learn to recognize their own pattern.

Sources: American Migraine Foundation: Migraine symptoms FAQ, NHS: Migraine, ICHD-3: Migraine with aura, The Migraine Trust: Stages of a migraine attack

What are common migraine triggers?

People most often report stress, too little or too much sleep, skipped meals, hormonal changes around periods, bright or flickering light, strong smells, alcohol, certain foods and drinks, changes in caffeine, and weather changes.

Triggers are personal. What sets off an attack for one person does nothing for another, and a trigger doesn’t bring on an attack every time, so your own history is a better guide than a generic list. Our guide to common migraine triggers has more.

Sources: American Migraine Foundation: Top 10 migraine triggers, NINDS: Migraine, Mayo Clinic: Migraine

Yes. HeadWell is a free headache and migraine tracker for iPhone, iPad and Android. You log attacks, early warning signs, triggers, periods and life events, see your history in a calendar and charts, and can create a PDF report for your doctor. The Features page lists everything it does.

We don’t sell your data, there are no ads, and we don’t use third-party analytics or advertising trackers. The full details are in the Privacy Policy.

Which headache symptoms are an emergency?

Get emergency help straight away (call 911, 999, 112 or your local emergency number) for:

  • a sudden, severe headache that reaches its worst within a minute (a “thunderclap” headache);
  • a headache with a high fever or a stiff neck, or with a rash that doesn’t fade when you press a glass against it;
  • a headache after a blow to the head;
  • a headache with confusion, severe drowsiness, weakness, numbness, trouble speaking, double vision or loss of vision, trouble walking or balancing, a seizure or fainting, when it’s sudden or new to you;
  • the worst headache of your life, or a sudden headache that feels completely different from your usual ones.

Get same-day medical advice (an urgent clinic or your local urgent-advice line) if a headache:

  • is worse after coughing, straining, bending or exercise;
  • comes with jaw pain when you chew, or a tender scalp, especially if you’re over 50;
  • comes with eye pain or vision problems;
  • is new during pregnancy or just after giving birth;
  • keeps getting steadily worse over days or weeks;
  • is new, and you have cancer, HIV or a weakened immune system;
  • belongs to a migraine attack that has lasted more than 72 hours, or comes with an aura that lasts more than an hour or is your first.
